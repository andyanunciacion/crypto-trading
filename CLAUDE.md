# crypto-trading

A self-hosted web app that watches crypto markets, runs swappable trading strategies and places orders automatically, with a risk layer that can't be bypassed. A personal tool for one operator, trading spot on MEXC from the Philippines (ARCHITECTURE D21).

## Hard rules
- **Every order goes through the RiskManager.** No bypass flags, no direct broker calls.
- **Strategies return signals only.** They never place orders or touch the exchange.
- **Only `ExchangeAdapter` imports CCXT.** Only the engine talks to the exchange; API and UI send commands via Redis.
- **Default mode is PAPER.** LIVE needs an explicit config flag and a confirmation.
- **Never enable or use withdrawal endpoints.**
- **Never commit secrets.** Keys come from environment variables; exchange keys are encrypted at rest.
- **Every order has a unique client order ID.**
- **Use Decimal, not float**, for prices, amounts, fees, balances and P&L (floats only inside indicator math).

Strategies live in `engine/src/engine/strategies/`: a stateless `Strategy` base (`on_candle(candles, position)` → `Signal`, deciding on exactly the last `warmup_candles`, D28), hand-rolled float indicators (EMA, Wilder's RSI, Bollinger Bands, Wilder's ATR), three strategies (`EmaCrossover`, `RsiMeanReversion`, `BollingerBreakout`; entries on events, exits on states, D31), and a registry mapping stored names back to classes. An event-driven backtester (`engine/src/engine/backtest/`, D29) replays stored candles through a strategy, filling each signal at the next candle's open with fees, slippage and minimum order sizes, and stores every run in `backtest_runs` with a hash of its candles so it can be reproduced. `report.py` turns a run's result into metrics (total return, max drawdown, Sharpe, trades, win rate, profit factor), computed when shown rather than stored (D30). `sweep.py` backtests every combination of a param grid over the same candles and ranks them by Sharpe; `walk_forward.py` picks the best set on each rolling train window and tests it unchanged on the window after, reporting in-sample and out-of-sample side by side (D32). `ExchangeAdapter` for MEXC exists (`engine/src/engine/exchange/`), tested offline against recorded responses; its reads retry network errors with backoff, orders are never auto-retried (D27). Candles are stored in a TimescaleDB hypertable (schema in `shared/src/shared/db/`, Alembic migrations applied by the one-shot `migrate` service); a backfill CLI (`engine/src/engine/marketdata/`) downloads history and finds/fills gaps. The engine streams live prices for `PRICE_SYMBOLS` into Redis (`price:<exchange>:<symbol>` key + `prices:<exchange>:<symbol>` channel, D26), over CCXT's WebSocket, with REST polling while it reconnects. The live signal path: `CandleStream` (`marketdata/candle_stream.py`) uses the WebSocket kline stream only to tell when a candle closed, then fetches it over REST, so live candles equal stored ones (D33). `SignalRunner` (`strategies/runner.py`) feeds them to a strategy over its warm-up window. A parity test replays a recorded 3-hour MEXC session through both and the backtester and requires identical signals. Brokers live in `engine/src/engine/brokers/` (D34): a `Broker` interface and a `PaperBroker` that fills limit orders at the live best bid/ask from Redis, capped by the size on offer there (partial fills), with the backtest fee. An order that crossed when placed keeps filling at the best price while it still crosses; a resting one fills at its limit. The size at a best price counts once (what our fills took there is remembered in memory), so a repeated quote doesn't fill again. It reserves funds for open orders, and stores everything in the `orders`, `fills`, `positions` and `paper_balances` tables (migration 0003). Client order IDs are deterministic per bot, candle and side, and the unique column makes a retried order return the first one. Money logic is pure and separately tested (`matching.py`, `ledger.py`). Only the `RiskManager` (`engine/src/engine/risk/manager.py`, D35) calls a broker. `submit()` logs `risk.decision` with the order's inputs, then places the order and stores the decision in its `reason`; `cancel()` is never blocked. Its limits (D41, `risk/limits.py`, pure) check every buy against `risk_settings` (one row per mode, NULL = off, migration 0008 seeds §6's values): max drawdown, max daily loss (then no buys until midnight in Manila, even after a bounce), max open positions (open buys count), each one a block, and max exposure per coin, which shrinks the buy to fit, or blocks it if what fits is too small. A sell or a stop's exit is never checked. The limits are account-wide: `risk/account.py` works out equity, peak, the day's start and exposure from every paper bot's balances, positions and open orders on each buy (stopped bots count), storing nothing. Every block or resize goes to `risk_events` before the broker is called. A retried client order ID gets its first answer, and buys are decided one at a time. Circuit breakers (D42, `risk/breakers.py`, settings in `risk_settings` from migration 0009) are checked before the limits, and block buys only:
- a bot that isn't `running` (read from `bots` on every buy);
- stale data: a price for the coin bought, or any coin held, older than 2 of the bot's candles;
- a price spike: the mid swung 3%+ within 5 min, from `PriceSwings`, which `PaperDesk.on_price` fills in memory.

The error breaker counts each bot's rejected or raising orders in memory. The 3rd within 10 min sets `bots.status = 'paused'` with `paused_reason` and logs `risk.breaker.tripped`. Paused bots still load (`load_bots`), sell and keep their stops; only their buys are blocked until they're set running again. The engine also streams prices for every coin a paper position holds. `test_broker_boundary.py` fails if anything outside `engine/brokers/` and `engine/risk/` imports more than the broker's data types, so whatever builds a `PaperBroker` lives in `engine/risk/`. The bot runner (`engine/src/engine/bots/`, D36) runs the `running` and `paused` bots from the `bots` and `strategy_configs` tables (migration 0004, which also points the trading tables' `bot_id` at `bots`). It loads them at engine startup, and the Bots page starts more while it runs (`BotRunner.add`, D47). Bots on a symbol/timeframe share one `CandleStream`. On each closed candle the candle is stored, then each bot cancels its leftover orders (an order lives one candle), gets its signal and, on BUY/SELL, submits a marketable limit order to the RiskManager. `orders.py` (pure) prices it at the ask/bid ± the backtest's slippage; a sell sells the whole position. Position sizing (D39) is one pure function, `entry_amount()` in `engine/risk/sizing.py`, which the bot runner and the backtester both call, so paper sizes exactly like its backtests. Its modes are `all_in` (default), `fixed`, `percent_of_equity` and `atr_risk` (lose `risk_percent` of equity at a stop `multiple` × ATR below). Each is scaled by `size_hint`, rounded down, capped at the free cash and refused below the market's minimums. A bot's sizing is JSON in `bots.sizing` and a backtest's in `backtest_runs.sizing` (migration 0005); each buy's `reason` records how it was sized. The RiskManager's limits cap what sizing asks for: by default no buy takes a coin past 25% of account equity, all-in or not. Stops (D40) are pure rules in `engine/risk/stops.py`: a stop-loss (percent or ATR below entry), a take-profit (percent, or an R multiple of the stop's distance) and a trailing stop (percent or ATR below the highest price since entry, never lowered), all placed at entry. Backtests take them per run (`--stops`, `backtest_runs.stops`, migration 0006) and check each candle pessimistically: a gap fills at the open, the stop is assumed before the take-profit in the same candle, and the trailing stop follows a candle's high only after that candle. Stop fills carry `exit`. Paper bots take them too (`bots.stops`, migration 0007; `bots/exits.py`): a buy records its stops and the ATR at its signal in its `reason`, and a buy whose stops can't be placed is skipped. Once it fills, the levels are placed from the position's `avg_entry` (again if a later slice moves it) and kept on the `positions` row, so a restart keeps them. `BotRunner.on_price` runs on every price update after the PaperBroker's fills (`PaperDesk.feed_prices(then=...)`). The trailing stop follows the bid. A bid at a level cancels the bot's open orders and sells the whole position through the RiskManager (client order ID `<buy id minus b>x<n>`), and keeps selling until it's gone; an exit the bid fell below is replaced. A bot's candle and its prices take turns (one lock per bot). A bot's error is logged and never stops the others. `PaperDesk` (`engine/risk/paper.py`) builds the one shared PaperBroker, funds bots, and feeds it live prices from the Redis channels so resting orders fill. The quote reader is `engine/marketdata/quotes.py`. Restart recovery (D38): at startup, before prices are fed, `BotRunner.recover()` cancels every open paper order (stopped bots' too) and logs each bot's state from the database (`bot.recovered`); the newest closed candle only seeds the window. A bot acts only on a candle that closed less than one timeframe step ago; older ones (an outage's backlog) fill its window and cancel its leftovers (`bot.caught_up`).

The kill switch (D43, `engine/risk/kill.py`) is a `KillCommand` (`shared/commands.py`) on the Redis list `engine:commands`. The CLI `engine.control.kill` sends it, and it needs only Redis, so it works with the API down. The engine's `CommandListener` (`engine/control/listener.py`) drains the list at startup before loading any bot, then pops as commands come, and replies on `engine:reply:<id>`. In order:
1. Every running or paused bot is set `stopped`, with `stopped_reason`/`stopped_at` (migration 0010); from then on the breaker blocks their buys.
2. `BotRunner.stop()` lets each bot finish what it's doing, then it never acts again.
3. Every open order is cancelled.
4. With `--close-positions`, every position is sold until it's gone: exits `<buy>x<n>` with kind `kill_switch`, carrying on a stop's numbering, and placed again when the bid falls below (`next_exit` in `bots/exits.py`). A restart carries on selling.

Blocking pops wait at most 1 s and loop, because redis-py gives up on a read after 5 s. The price feed listens to the bots' coins and every held coin; it runs the stops, then the kill switch's sells. A drawdown breach doesn't trip the kill switch (manual only, D43).

The Bots page (D47, `/bots`, `backend/bots/`, `engine/control/bots.py`):
- **Commands, not writes:** `POST /bots`, `/bots/{id}/start`, `/pause`, `/stop` (`close_position`) send a `CreateBotCommand`/`BotCommand` on the kill switch's list (`shared/commands.py`, one discriminated `Command`) and return the engine's `BotReply`: a refusal is a 422 with its reason, no reply in 10 s a 504. Bot commands expire then (`expires_at`); the listener drops an expired one unanswered. A kill never expires.
- **`BotControl`:** create checks everything the CLI does and adds the bot **stopped**, funded. Start (refused while the kill switch sells its position) sets it running and calls `runner.add()`; pause sets `paused`, as the error breaker does; stop sets `stopped`, stops it in the runner, cancels its orders, and with `close_position` sells it until gone (`KillSwitch.close_bot`, exits of kind `close`, resumed after a restart). With no runner yet (draining at startup), only the database changes.
- **Starting while running:** `BotRunner.run()` stays up with no bots. `add()` warms the bot up in the background, then seeds it, in one step, with the candles its series delivered meanwhile; a series that isn't streaming gets a new stream task. `Prices.ensure` (`main.py`) streams a new symbol. The heartbeat's `bots` lists those warmed up.
- **The catalog:** the engine `SET`s `engine:catalog` at startup (`shared/catalog.py`: each strategy, sizing mode and stop kind, every setting a `FieldSpec` read from its pydantic model); `GET /strategies` serves it. `StrategyParamsForm` draws it; decimals are sent as strings.

Switching strategy (D51, P6-1): Edit on the Bots page (`EditStrategySheet`) sends a `SwitchStrategyCommand` via `POST /bots/{id}/strategy`.
- **The command:** `BotControl._switch` checks the params, appends a `strategy_configs` row, points the bot at it and stores the policy in `bots.switch_policy`. A policy alone adds no config. A stopped bot changes in the database only.
- **The swap:** `BotRunner.switch()` warms the new config up from REST in the background. It takes over at the bot's next candle, seeded with every candle before it; until then the old config acts.
- **The policies** (for an open position):
  - `keep`: the opening buy's `reason.strategy_config` is the bot's `keeper` (rebuilt by `load_bots`), and only its SELL is acted on until the bot is flat.
  - `close_first`: `KillSwitch.close_bot(kind="switch")` sells it until it's gone, while the runner holds the bot (`selling=kill_switch.sells_for`).
  - `hand_over`: the new config sells it.

  The trade keeps its stops under all three.

Config history and rollback (D52, P6-2, `/bots/[id]`):
- **Append-only, enforced:** a trigger (migration 0013) refuses any UPDATE or DELETE on `strategy_configs`, so tests that need an odd config insert one and point the bot at it.
- **The page:** a bot's name on the Bots list opens it. `GET /bots/{id}` gives its setup and every config, newest first, numbered per bot. Roll back on an earlier one (`RollbackDialog`, with the policy radios `SwitchPolicyFields` shared with Edit) sends a `RollbackCommand` via `POST /bots/{id}/rollback`.
- **The rollback:** `BotControl._rollback` reads the config (this bot's only), builds it at its stored version (refused if the strategy moved on), and switches through the same `_apply_switch` as Edit. It adds a row copying it, with `restored_from` = its id. Rolling back to the params it runs now is refused.

The regime detector (D53, P6-3, `engine/regime/detector.py`):
- **Pure and stateless, like a strategy:** `RegimeDetector.detect(candles)` gives a `RegimeReading` (regime, ts, reason, indicators) from exactly its last `warmup_candles` (199 with the defaults). `Regime` and `RegimeParams` live in `shared/regime.py`.
- **The rules, in order:**
  1. ATR% ≥ 1.5 × its 100-candle average → `high_volatility`.
  2. ADX(14) ≥ 25, with +DI/−DI and the EMA(50)'s 10-candle slope agreeing → `trending_up`/`trending_down`; disagreeing → `unclear`.
  3. ADX < 20 → `ranging`.
  4. Otherwise `unclear`, a dead band the switcher should hold through. `adx()` is in `shared/indicators.py`.
- **Tested on history:** `test_regime_history.py` replays Binance BTC/USDT 1d, 2019–2023 (`engine.regime.record_history`; MEXC keeps a year) over nine labeled spans. Trend and range spans are judged from 2 × `adx_period` candles in, because ADX recognises a new regime weeks late on 1d.

Auto-switching (D54, P6-4, `engine/regime/switcher.py`, the bot's page):
- **Per bot:** `bots.auto_switch` (migration 0014, `shared/regime.AutoSwitchSettings`, NULL = off) maps each regime but `unclear` to a strategy config, with `min_hold` (default 3) and a switch policy. `stay_flat` is a strategy (SELL while holding, else HOLD) for "no position". Set by an `AutoSwitchCommand` (`POST /bots/{id}/auto-switch`, `AutoSwitchSheet`); the engine checks each config, stores its version, and reloads a running bot so it keeps the regime's window.
- **The rule, pure and stateless:** `AutoSwitcher.check()` reads the last `min_hold` candles' regimes, each over its own window. All the same and not `unclear` confirms it; if the bot doesn't run that regime's config, it switches. The active config is the only state, so a restart or a backtest decides alike, and two switches are at least `min_hold` candles apart.
- **The switch:** after each live candle, `BotRunner._regime` logs `bot.regime` and hands a switch to `BotControl.auto_switch` in the background. That goes through Edit's `_apply_switch` (trigger `regime`, `created_by` `regime`), dropped under the row lock if the bot stopped, its settings changed or it already runs the config. One at a time per bot.
- **Manual switches:** Edit and rollback are refused while it's on.
- **The record:** every switch (`manual`, `rollback`, `regime` with the regime, confirming candle and reading) is a row in `strategy_switches` (append-only, a trigger), written with the config. 0014 recorded the earlier switches from the config history. The chart marks them (`switches` on `GET`/`WS /bots/{id}/chart`; a new one reloads the chart) and the bot's page lists them as its switch log.
- **Done-when:** `test_auto_switch_replay.py` replays the BTC 1d fixture: every switch comes after `min_hold` identical readings, with a reason (57/41/34 switches for a hold of 1/3/5).

Auto-switching backtests (D55, P6-5, `engine/backtest/simulator.py`, `compare.py`):
- **Inside `run_backtest()`:** `BacktestConfig.auto_switch` (`--auto-switch` on `engine.backtest.run`; `with_versions` in `regime/switcher.py` fills each config's version, for `BotControl` too). The strategy given is the one it starts on.
- **As live:** after each candle's signal, `AutoSwitcher.check()` runs at that candle, and a switch takes over at the next. `keep` acts only on the opening buy's config's SELL. `close_first` sells at the next open (exit `switch`), after a same-candle BUY. `hand_over` lets the new config sell. Stops carry over.
- **The record:** `switches` (`BacktestSwitch`) and each fill's `decided_by` in the result; `backtest_runs.auto_switch` and `regime_detector_version` (migration 0015). `reproduce()` refuses a moved-on detector.
- **The report:** the mapping, the switches and each config's candles, trades and P&L. `run` also prints `compare.py`'s baselines (each config alone and buy-and-hold, not stored). The Backtests page labels these runs and lists their switches.
- **Found:** on BTC 1d 2019–2023 it lost to every config alone (EMA +641%, it −5% to −30%). EMA's cross entry comes before the late trend confirmation; that's P6-5b's subject.

The detector lag study (D56, P6-5b, `engine/regime/study.py`):
- **Nothing promoted:** the detector stays at version 1 and EMA crossover keeps its cross entry.
- **The study:** `python -m engine.regime.study FILE...` runs four detector variants (v1, Kaufman's efficiency ratio in ADX's place, an efficiency-ratio trend exit, ADX(10)) and a state entry for EMA (`EmaStateEntry`, not registered). They live only in the study.
  - It prints a lag/flip report (`regime/lag.py`) and the combined backtest against the baselines, on BTC 1d 2019–2023 and two holdouts (ETH 2019–2023, BTC 2024–2026).
  - It then applies a keep rule fixed in advance: beat v1 in ≥ 6 of 9 hold × policy cells, and ≥ 2 of 3 on each holdout.
- **Found:**
  - The efficiency ratio saw spans 1–3 weeks sooner and passed the rule, but it failed 3 of the 9 labeled spans: it ignores how big a move is, so a quiet drift reads as a trend.
  - The state entry was the bigger lever (v1 +162% to +456% on BTC 2019–2023) but lost every cell on BTC 2024–2026.
  - Nothing beat its baselines.
- **Also:** `efficiency_ratio()` is in `shared/indicators.py`, the labeled spans are in `regime/history.py`, and `record_history` takes `--symbol/--start/--end`.

The Backtests page (D48, `/backtests`, `backend/backtests/`, `engine/backtest/worker.py`):
- **The backtester service:** a 6th Compose service, `backtester` (the engine's image), runs the dashboard's backtests one at a time, so a long run never holds up the engine. `POST /backtests` queues a `RunBacktestCommand` on `backtest:commands` (`shared/backtest_jobs.py`) and answers 202 with the job. The browser polls `GET /backtests/jobs/{id}` (`queued` → `running` → `done` with `run_id`, or `failed` with the reason). Backtest commands never expire.
- **The CLI's run, exactly:** the worker builds the config with the CLI's `RunSettings` + `build_config()` (`backtest/cli.py`) and runs and stores with `run.backtest()`, so a dashboard run equals `engine.backtest.run` with the same arguments (`test_backtest_worker.py`, `e2e/backtests.spec.ts`).
- **Metrics in `shared/`:** `shared/backtest.py` holds the fill/equity/trade/report models and `metrics()`; the engine's `report()` calls it, and the API reports on stored runs with it (`GET /backtests`, `/backtests/{id}`). `GET /candles/range` says what's stored of a series.
- **Frontend:** `BacktestForm` (with `StrategySetup`, shared with `NewBotSheet`), the runs list, and `/backtests/[id]` with metric tiles, `EquityChart` (drawdown in its own pane), trades and skipped orders. Fractions become percents with `shiftPoint` (`lib/format.ts`), never a float.

History and Logs (D49, `/history`, `/logs`, `backend/history/`, `backend/logs/`):
- **History:** `GET /history/orders` and `/history/fills` (filters: bot, symbol, side, kind, status, a `start` ≤ t < `end` range; paged by id) and their `.csv` exports, every matching row streamed with the database's exact values (NUMERIC via `format(d, "f")`, never `1E-8`; UTC times to the microsecond). The page's From/To are Manila days, sent as a UTC range; Export CSV uses the table's own query. A row's time opens `TradeExplainPanel`.
- **The log mirror:** `shared/logs.py`'s `LogMirror` (a structlog processor) copies INFO+ lines (not `engine.heartbeat`) from the engine, backtester and API into a 10,000-line buffer, and `ship()` `XADD`s them to the Redis stream `logs` (`MAXLEN ~ 50000`) every 0.5 s. Logging never waits on Redis; CLIs don't mirror.
- **Logs:** `GET /logs` (level and above, event prefixes, bot, service; paged by stream id) and `GET /risk-events` (the `risk_events` rows, with their inputs; blocked buys show only here).

Settings (D50, `/settings`, `backend/risk_settings/`, `backend/keys/`):
- **The API writes these itself**, not through the engine. The engine keeps no copy: it reads `risk_settings` on every buy, and nothing reads the keys until P9.
- **A code again:** loosening a limit (UX4) or saving exchange keys needs a current TOTP code, checked by `confirm_code` (`backend/auth/login.py`) in the change's own transaction.

## Commands
Only list commands that exist. First run: copy `.env.example` to `.env`, set a database password, and make `JWT_SECRET` and `ENCRYPTION_KEY` with the one-liners there. Then create the dashboard user (below).
- `docker compose up --build -d`: build and start all six services; `docker compose ps -a` should show each one `(healthy)`, plus `migrate` as `Exited (0)` (it applies database migrations, then stops). After adding a migration use this, not `docker compose build engine`: `migrate` has its own image, and a stale one skips the new migration. The same goes for engine code the dashboard's backtests run: `backtester` has its own image too, so rebuild it with `up --build` (or `docker compose build backtester`)
- `docker compose run --rm migrate`: apply database migrations by hand (`up` already does this)
- `docker compose run --rm engine python -m engine.bots.create btc-ema ema_crossover BTC/USDT 1h --params '{"fast": 9, "slow": 21}' --cash 1000`: create a running paper bot with that strategy config and paper cash in the quote currency (checks the params and that the exchange lists the symbol). `--sizing '{"mode": "percent_of_equity", "percent": 25}'` sets how much each buy spends (default all-in; also `{"mode": "fixed", "amount": 100}` and `{"mode": "atr_risk", "risk_percent": 1, "period": 14, "multiple": 2}`). `--stops` takes the same JSON as a backtest's (below; default none). The engine loads bots when it starts, so then `docker compose restart engine`, or press Start on the Bots page (no restart). The Bots page (http://localhost:3000/bots) does all of this from a form, creating the bot stopped, and starts, pauses and stops bots. Its Edit switches a bot's strategy or params while it runs, with a policy for its open position (keep / close first / hand over). A bot's name opens its detail page (http://localhost:3000/bots/1), which lists every config it has had and rolls back to any earlier one; `SELECT id, bot_id, strategy, params, created_by, created_at, restored_from FROM strategy_configs ORDER BY id` lists them too (a rollback's row has `restored_from`; the table refuses UPDATE and DELETE)
- `docker compose run --rm engine python -m engine.marketdata.backfill BTC/USDT 1h 4h --days 365`: download candles and fill gaps (exit 1 if gaps remain); add `--check` to only report gaps. MEXC keeps ~30 days of 1m and ~360 days of 5m/15m, so use `--days 30` for 1m
- `docker compose run --rm engine python -m engine.backtest.run ema_crossover BTC/USDT 1h --days 360`: backtest on stored candles, save the run to `backtest_runs` and print its metrics. The range ends at the last stored candle; warm-up comes from before it, so `--days` must leave room for it. Options: `--params '{"fast": 9, "slow": 21}'`, `--fee-rate`, `--slippage`, `--cash`, `--sizing` (as for `bots.create`), `--stops '{"stop_loss": {"kind": "atr", "multiple": 2}, "take_profit": {"kind": "r", "multiple": 2}, "trailing": {"kind": "percent", "percent": 3}}'` (any of the three; `atr_period` defaults to 14). `sweep` and `walk_forward` take both too. `run` alone takes `--auto-switch '{"mapping": {"trending_up": {"strategy": "ema_crossover", "params": {"fast": 9, "slow": 21}}, "ranging": {"strategy": "rsi_mean_reversion"}, "trending_down": {"strategy": "stay_flat"}, "high_volatility": {"strategy": "stay_flat"}}, "min_hold": 3, "policy": "keep"}'`: it switches as a bot's auto-switching would, starting on the strategy given, needs the detector's 199 candles (+ `min_hold` − 1) of warm-up (on 1d, keep `--days` well under what's stored), and also prints each config alone and buy-and-hold over the same candles. The other strategies are `rsi_mean_reversion` (params `period`, `oversold`, `overbought`), `bollinger_breakout` (`period`, `width`, `volume_factor`) and `stay_flat` (no params; never buys, for auto-switching). After changing engine code, run `docker compose build engine` first, because `run` uses the built image
- `docker compose run --rm engine python -m engine.backtest.sweep ema_crossover BTC/USDT 1h --grid '{"fast": [5, 9, 12], "slow": [21, 30, 50]}' --days 90`: backtest every combination in the grid over the same range, print them ranked by Sharpe (plus combinations the params refuse, like fast ≥ slow), and store the winner. Takes `run`'s cost options. In Windows PowerShell 5.1, escape the JSON's inner quotes as `\"` (also for `--params`)
- `docker compose run --rm engine python -m engine.backtest.walk_forward ema_crossover BTC/USDT 1h --grid '...' --days 360 --train-days 90 --test-days 30`: pick the grid's best set on each 90-day window, test it on the 30 days after, roll forward; prints in-sample vs. out-of-sample per window and stores both runs of each. Needs warm-up candles before the first window, so keep `--days` a little under what's stored

## Engineering conventions
- Python 3.12+, fully typed; mypy strict and ruff clean. TypeScript `strict`; no `any`.
- Validate at boundaries with pydantic: env config, API inputs, exchange responses, strategy params.
- Trading logic lives in `engine/`; `backend/` only reads data and sends commands, except the settings the engine reads fresh and holds no copy of (`risk_settings`, `exchange_accounts`), which it writes itself (D50); `frontend/` is a client only.
- Timestamps are UTC (`timestamptz`); the UI shows Asia/Manila. Money is `NUMERIC` in Postgres and strings in JSON.
- Schema changes go through Alembic migrations only.
- Log every signal, risk decision, order and strategy switch with structlog, including its inputs. Never log keys, secrets or TOTP codes.
- Tests live in each package's `tests/`; strategies are tested on known price series; critical UI flows (login, bot control, kill switch) are tested with Playwright.

## Domain glossary
- **Candle**: OHLCV bar for one symbol and timeframe. Strategies act only on **closed** candles.
- **Signal**: a strategy's output for a candle: BUY, SELL or HOLD, with an optional size hint and stop.
- **Strategy**: a plug-in that turns market data into signals. It has a name, version, params and warm-up.
- **Strategy config**: a strategy plus specific params, versioned and append-only.
- **Bot**: one strategy config running on one symbol/timeframe/account, in `paper` or `live` mode.
- **Mode**: `backtest`, `paper` (fake balances, live prices) or `live` (real money).
- **Broker**: places orders. `PaperBroker` simulates fills; `LiveBroker` uses the exchange. Both share one interface.
- **RiskManager**: sits between signals and the broker; blocks, shrinks or allows every order.
- **Position sizing**: how much a buy spends (`risk/sizing.py`): all-in, fixed, percent of equity, or ATR risk. Shared by backtests and bots; the RiskManager's limits cap it.
- **ATR (Average True Range)**: how far the price typically moves in one candle, gaps included; bigger means more volatile.
- **Stop-loss / take-profit**: sell when the price falls to a level below the entry (caps the loss) or rises to one above it (banks the gain).
- **Trailing stop**: a stop that follows the highest price since entry at a set distance and never moves down, so it keeps part of a gain.
- **R multiple**: a gain or target measured in units of what the stop-loss risks (entry − stop); a 2R take-profit aims to win twice the risk.
- **Kill switch**: cancels orders, stops all bots, optionally closes positions.
- **Circuit breaker**: automatic pause on stale data, repeated errors or price spikes.
- **Regime**: market state (trending up/down, ranging, high volatility) used for auto-switching.
- **Slippage**: the difference between the expected price and the fill price.
- **Drawdown**: the fall in equity from its peak.
- **Walk-forward**: optimize on one window, test on the next, then roll forward, to catch overfitting.
- **Reconciliation**: on startup, comparing engine records with the exchange and fixing mismatches before trading.
- **Client order ID**: our own unique ID on each order, so retries never duplicate.
